01 / Profile

A career across disciplines.

Dr. Liang’s work is rooted in medicine, but his career has continually expanded the tools used to understand disease.

He began his career in clinical medicine in Jilin, China, where he practiced neurology before relocating to Canada. His graduate training in biochemistry and medical microbiology led into viral informatics, next-generation sequencing and computational biology. Today, he leads research at the National Microbiology Laboratory while also teaching and supervising research as a professor at the University of Manitoba.

The through-line is not a change away from medicine, but a widening of perspective: from treating individual patients to studying the molecular, genomic and population-level patterns that shape infectious disease.

03 / Research

Research focus.

His work sits at the boundary between biological science and computation, using genomic data to understand how viruses evolve, how hosts respond and where new interventions may be possible.

Research area 01

Viral genomics & bioinformatics

Sequencing, computational analysis and large biological datasets used to characterize viral diversity, evolution and pathogenesis.

Research area 02

HIV drug resistance & surveillance

Next-generation sequencing and informatics approaches supporting HIV genetic characterization and drug-resistance surveillance.

Research area 03

Host–virus interactions

Human genetic determinants and biological responses associated with susceptibility, resistance and disease severity.

Research area 04

Vaccines & therapeutics

Preclinical vaccine strategies and computational approaches to infectious-disease prevention and treatment.
